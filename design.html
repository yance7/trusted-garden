02 / Design basis

The model borrows logic, not decoration.

The spatial, biological and technical reference points behind the model.

Explore the 3D farm ↗

Maize geometry

Field rows use an approximately 0.76 m reference spacing, a common 30-inch research and production spacing cited by University of Minnesota Extension. Plant density is visually decimated for browser performance.

Soil sensing

Each plot shows representative paired-depth soil sensing rather than a single “smart stick,” reflecting extension guidance to monitor more than one root-zone depth and more than one representative field location.

Aquaponics

The east greenhouse combines a round fish system, aeration, solids separation, biofiltration, sump and deep-water-culture beds—an intentionally legible loop derived from FAO small-scale aquaponics guidance.

Visitor choreography

A straight public/research spine, transparent growing spaces and framed views borrow planning lessons from agricultural research centers where education routes are integrated with working production and research areas.

Robotic phenotyping

USDA-ARS projects describe autonomous field robots that combine GPS/IMU, LiDAR, computer vision and modular sensor payloads, as well as multimodal field phenotyping and UAV sensing. V0.5 turns those research patterns into a visible reference-farm equipment layer.

Edge + machine vision

USDA-ARS has reported edge-computing video analysis for UAV crop monitoring, while commercial precision systems use camera-to-processor-to-actuator loops for site-specific operations. The model therefore shows field compute, machine-vision nodes and explicit actuator endpoints rather than a generic “AI cloud.”

Trusted Garden is a conceptual reference farm. Operating readings, intervention examples and evidence trails are simulated; they do not describe a connected physical installation.
